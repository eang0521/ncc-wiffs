Rules & Physics

Field

  • Grass field with a square infield: 40 ft between every base (2nd base sits ~57 ft from home).
  • PVC strike zone: 23" wide × 28" tall, bottom 17" off the ground, set 4 ft behind home plate. Mound is 35 ft from the zone.
  • 7×7 ft backstop 4 ft behind the zone acts as the "catcher".
  • A 4-ft-tall fence in one smooth arc from foul line to foul line: five equal-angle panels whose centers sit at 60, 65, 70, 65, 60 ft (left to right), about 57.5 ft at the foul lines.
  • Bunt line 10 ft in front of home, straight across.

Play

  • 4 v 4: P, 1B, SS, OF. Everyone bats. No catcher. Fielders shade by batter handedness and power.
  • If the batter due up is on base, the player who made the last out takes his place on that base.
  • 4 balls / 3 strikes. Foul balls are strikes, but with two strikes you can only strike out on a foul tip that goes through the zone.
  • A pitch passing through the frame or clipping the PVC is a strike. A pitch that bounces first is a ball.
  • Anything fielded (or stopping) short of the bunt line is foul.
  • Runners actually run the bases. No leading off. Tag-ups, pegging (hitting a runner off base with a thrown ball) and force outs all apply.
  • A throw home that hits the PVC zone or the backstop retires the runner heading home.
  • Over the fence on the fly = home run. Bounces over = ground-rule double. Off the fence = live ball (not a catch).
  • Speed limit: radar 63–64 mph is a warning (one per half inning); the next one, or anything 65+, is an automatic ball. If an over-the-limit pitch is put in play, the play stands.
  • 3 innings, no mercy rule; extra innings are played as normal innings.

Physics model

  • Ball: 0.75 oz perforated plastic ball, ~9" circumference, quadratic drag (Cd ≈ 0.40), RK2 integration. Wiffle balls slow down hard: a 50 mph pitch reaches the zone around 30 mph.
  • Pitch movement comes from an aerodynamic side force in the pitcher's frame that ramps in late. Pitch type, pitch level (1–10), movement rating, arm slot and fatigue all set the break. Pitchers aim using the break they expect; the real break varies, so command and movement interact.
  • Batting: the batter perceives location and timing with errors driven by Contact, Eye, pitch break, speed differential (off-speed deception) and familiarity. The bat is a thin cylinder swept around the batter's body; the bat–ball collision uses impulse physics (bat effective mass, COR falling off away from the sweet spot, friction). Pop-ups, grounders, jams, foul tips and barrels all emerge from the collision.
  • Familiarity: every pitch a batter sees of a given type from a given pitcher raises his recognition of it (up to about +36% eye). Managers (AI or you) can swap pitchers between batters to reset it, at a cost if the reliever is weaker.
  • Fatigue: velocity, command and movement fade slightly once a pitcher passes his stamina budget.
  • Batted balls flutter (random side force), bounce irregularly on grass, roll with rolling resistance, and carom off fence panels and the backstop.
  • Fielders predict the ball's path, choose the best interceptor, and react, accelerate and catch based on ratings. Throws are physically simulated (heavy drag makes long throws slow) with accuracy error. The fielder picks the best play: force, throw home at the zone/backstop, peg a runner, tag, or relay.
  • Runners estimate the defense's time to the next base and take extra bases, retreat or tag up accordingly.
